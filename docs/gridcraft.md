# Gridcraft language

Grid puzzles are authored in the puzzle editor and played in the grid puzzle view. Use these words in code, comments, and the editor.

A **level** is one blueprint step (`GridStep`). A **blueprint**, also called a **craft**, is the ordered list of levels (`GridBlueprint`). Both words mean that list. `CraftV1` and the 3D crafting blueprints are a different system. The puzzle editor groups blueprint files into puzzle-collection folders. A level is still a blueprint step.

In play, a **collection** is that folder, and a **puzzle** is one blueprint in the folder. Puzzles run in collection order: name, then id. The grid puzzle view opens both from two buttons. Finishing a puzzle slides the next one in from offscreen right, then drops the finished puzzle and puts the new one back at the coordinates it was authored with. Finishing the last puzzle shows the victory screen for the collection. A collection or a puzzle can be **hidden** (`"hidden": true` in `collection.json` or the puzzle file): play leaves it out, and the puzzle editor still lists it, marked hidden.

A **blueprint piece** is one closed ring on a level. It is a separate part. A tool cannot cross its boundary. Paper may be cut along a penned edge, up to that edge, and onward only when the next collinear edge allows it. An open polyline is also a valid drawn shape: it is not joined back to its first point, and the blade still stops on it. A **paper piece** is a `PapercutPiece` on the runtime sheet. Do not call a paper piece a blueprint piece.

An **attachment** changes how a tool behaves. It is authored on the level. The player does not toggle it during play.

A **level permutation** changes the level’s environment or what the player can see and select. Darkness, mirror axes, and no-fold zones are permutations.

A **collectible** is an entity a tool must collect to pass the level. Color gems and number gems are collectibles. Color gems are collected in groups: the first color opens that group, and taking another color before the group is finished fails the level. A cut can still start anywhere on the paper edge. Number gems are the entry points onto the paper: while one remains, a new cut starts at a number gem, and the blade can return to the paper only at one. A gem disappears when the blade takes it. Every gem must be taken before the level is cleared. The gem, piece, or other entity that causes a failure flashes red, then the level restarts.

A **failure condition** restores the level to its state before any moves: the sheet, cuts, folds, collectibles, tool-use counters, and rotation. The camera then returns to the pan, zoom, and roll the level opened with. Resetting the puzzle and restoring that starting position are separate steps. Clearing the puzzle leaves the camera where it is.

By default a cut that makes two or more paper pieces keeps every new piece, and they step apart. **Discard failure** is a level option, off by default. With it on, the piece that still holds an unsolved blueprint piece is kept. An exact cut-out of a blueprint piece stays. Every other new piece is scrap: it fades away. Paper that only shares a blueprint edge, with none of that blueprint inside it, is scrap too, and cutting it away is not a failure. When two new pieces each still hold an unsolved blueprint piece, that is a discarded-blueprint failure: the smallest new paper piece flashes red, then the level restarts. At victory, paper that is still on the sheet and is not a freed blueprint piece is counted in unit cells, one point each, into a score that lasts for the collection.

Tools start with unlimited uses. A level may set a **tool filter** that lists the tools which are available and, optionally, how many uses each one has. Each tool defines what a use means. Scissors spend grid-unit length along the cut. The folder spends one use per fold or unfold. A press scores a crease. Holding still arms a fold: drag past the edge-on marks to fold the opposite side over, and release to finish or let it fall flat. The crease runs only between cuts and paper edges. The hole punch spends one use per punch. Select and quarter-turn rotation do not spend uses.

A level may link a **folded model**: the 3D shape its paper pieces wrap (`FoldedModel`). The link is `"craft": {"id": ..., "step": ...}` on the level, naming a version-1 craft (`CraftV1`) and one of its crafting steps. The cube, the box stack, and the side table share that schema; `tool/generate_cube_craft.dart` generates the cube's craft and the box stack's craft and level. The box stack, the first level in the play library, is two cube crosses on one sheet. Each folds into its own island, the boxes stand stacked, and the top box is turned 15°. The Papercraft collection holds three crafts described in 3D and unfolded by `tool/generate_papercrafts.dart` (`unfoldPapercraft`): a rocket (a body, a pyramid nose, a flared skirt, and two fins, in five islands), a birdhouse (a base, a gabled house, a perch, and a roof panel), and a coffee cup (an open flared cup, a sleeve round its waist, a lid, and its sip bump). Each is a pattern level whose paper is printed with its net once, fold lines included. Every net vertex lands on a whole grid unit, no two faces overlap, and each island's outline is one loop. Where one part stands on a face, that face is split into a frame round its footprint, so every edge the part meets is a whole edge. Each frame unfolds as a U, open on one side, so the cut round an island passes each point once. The sheet's +z folds toward each island's middle, as in the cube. A **model face** is one flat face of the folded model. It belongs to the blueprint piece that contains it: for the cube each square is its own face, and for the side table one closed ring holds several faces split by penciled fold lines. An **island** is the set of model faces one paper piece covers. The grid puzzle view has a 2D/3D toggle when the link resolves.

Paper always lies on a plane or sits in the **inventory**, a bar labelled along its inner edge. It floats in from the right while paper can move into or out of it: with the select tool on the flat desk, while desk paper is selected in 3D, and over a model face once it holds paper. The **desk** is the plane the 2D view crafts on: the world sheet, outlined in 3D as a rectangle around the blueprint's paper. Every model face is a plane too. Cut paper stays on the desk until it is dragged onto the inventory, with the select tool in 2D or selected in 3D. Dragging it out of the inventory lays it back on the desk. Laying it on a face takes it out of the inventory, and undo in 3D puts it back. Tools never reach paper in the inventory or on the model. In 3D the dot grid fades, the model fades in floating above the middle of the desk, and the desk paper turns a faint grey. A **decal** is a label printed on paper (`Decal`). It belongs to the paper whose outline holds its center: it slides, turns, stows, and wraps with that paper, and with no paper under it, it is not drawn. It lies flat in the paper's plane, so its orientation is fixed to the paper and the model rather than the screen, and from behind it is flipped to read forwards. Each island's root face carries a letter decal, and so does the face most nearly opposite the root, if any: A and D on the cube's base and lid. Letters follow face order. Each lettered model face shows its letter faintly, as where that paper wraps to. The paper has a front, so one letter is enough to give exactly one way to lay it.

The reticle sits in the middle of the screen in 3D. The **preselected** face is the model face, or the desk, under the reticle, with any pointer, since paper dragged in from the inventory lands there. It fills orange, fading in and out as the reticle moves. The **aim** of a tap is the cursor with a mouse, and the reticle with touch; DevTools' Input setting can force the PC cursor or the mobile reticle for any pointer. Once the camera has rested on it for a quarter second, the paper inventory slides in at the right. All 3D work happens from where the camera is: nothing flies onto a face. **Selection**: a tap selects the paper under the aim, on a face or on the desk. Selected paper is edged in white, the outline fading in over a quarter second and out again once the paper is let go; its fill does not change. Only selected paper moves: with paper selected, one finger dragging anywhere slides it on its plane by the drag; with nothing selected, one finger orbits. A tap off all paper, or a double-tap on selected paper, deselects it, and paper on a face stays where it lies. Two fingers always pan and zoom, as do the right and middle mouse buttons. A **view pan** moves only by the drag: the point under the pointer when it begins, or else the nearest face in view, keeps its distance from the camera and follows the pointer's moves, and a pinch zooms about it. While paper moves, its plane's dot grid shows at once, and it fades out once the paper is let go. Dropping a piece from the inventory, or tapping it there, lays it on the preselected plane and selects it. Desk paper shows as paper while the desk is preselected or its paper is selected, and releasing selected desk paper over the inventory stows it. The 2D/3D toggle goes back to the flat desk to craft. A **placement** is a paper piece laid on a face (`PiecePlacement`): slid by whole grid cells and turned by quarter turns. Selected paper on a face carries a rotate icon; tapping it opens a **gimbal**, a ring around the paper. Dragging the ring turns the paper about its middle, a quarter turn at a time, and turning back puts it where it was. Paper on the wrong face goes back to the inventory: **Stow**, or dragging the selected paper onto the inventory, takes it off the face. It matches when the paper is exactly an island's cut-out and every letter lands on its own model face. The face glows, and then the **wrap** folds the rest of the island around the model. After a wrap, the camera backs out past the model's framing, turns once around the model's vertical axis, and comes to rest where it backed out to. Then the island scores 100 points in the header. An island scores once per level, so undoing and wrapping it again adds nothing. When every island is wrapped, the model glows as a stand-in for completion. Undo in 3D takes back the last placement. Paper on the model is shaded by a fixed light, so paper facing a different way, as across a fold, reads as a different shade.

A level may set a **cut pattern** (`"pattern": {"period": ..., "segments": ..., "sheet": ...}`): its paper is **pattern paper**, ruled with `segments` tiled every `period` in the paper's own coordinates, and the level is played on the model only. Grid Cube in the Boxes collection is one. The sheet starts in the inventory, the blueprint is not drawn, and there is no desk and no 2D view. Pattern paper is see-through: a 40% fill with the pattern in the paper's own color at 44%, just showing over it, on the model, in hand, and in the inventory. A Select, Scissors, and Fold carousel sits over the model. Select selects, slides, and turns paper as usual, and switching to it drops the cut under way; Scissors and Fold share that cut. With Scissors, a tap sets the blade at the aim on the nearest pattern vertex, pattern line, or paper edge, taking up the paper there. Red rings on the paper in hand suggest **cut starts**: each vertex of the face the paper lies on that is on the paper. A tap on or near a ring, or with the aim near one, sets the blade there, and until the blade is down the start dot shows where a tap would set it; after that, small red dots mark where the blade can go next, at the next vertex of a pattern line or of the cut so far, fainter where it would only travel back along the cut, and a tap within 80 px of a dot takes that stroke, so a dot under the tool bar still answers; every 3 seconds a little circle fades in and out at each dot, in the order of those strokes; any other tap cuts along the pattern toward the side of the blade it lands on, to the next vertex. The dots hide while the blade moves. Back along the cut so far, the blade **travels** instead: it moves on, cutting nothing, and that stretch is dashed. A slide along the paper's own edge is not a way to go. While the scissors cut, the camera keeps its lens and look-at point and orbits, turning by the shortest way, to look square at the blade's face from the side the paper is on, turned 80% of the way from there toward the blade as seen from the middle of the model, enough to peek round the edge. It zooms on that lens so the blade and every place it can go next fit on screen, inside about 82% of the frame. DevTools' Camera settings set that share as Blade pull, from 0 (square to the face) to 100% (straight at the blade). Mid-cut, a drag **peeks**: the camera turns away from there, held back harder the further it goes up to about 43°, enough to see round to the neighboring faces, and springs back to following the blade on release. The cut is a glowing red line, with a red dot where it first cut. An **aligned cell** is a face lined up with the pattern on paper lying on the model, in hand or not: every edge of the face lies along pattern lines or paper edges, and the paper covers the face. Its cell on the paper fades over 0.75 s to an opaque green, until a patch covers the face. The lines joined to that cell, and not those of a separate piece of the pattern, take a white overlay that fades in with the cell: a line on the cell is fully white, and the overlay falls to 20% at the lines farthest from the cell along that piece. It stays opaque while other paper fades off the face being worked on. A cut does not fold the paper: past a model edge the blade cuts the overhang, flat. To **fold**, switch to Fold; the cut waits, its scissors at the blade, and taps cut nothing. A line dragged from the model is matched to the model edges the paper can fold over as it lies now: edges of the faces the paper is on, with a neighboring face across them, paper on both sides, and no fold there yet. The edge nearest the line on screen, within 40 px and 20° of it, glows faintly while dragging. On release, the paper past that edge's line folds onto the neighboring face, as a stroke crossing the edge would once have folded it, and the edge glows orange, in and out over 0.3 s, as the paper swings over. A line near no such edge does nothing. Back on Scissors, the cut carries on from the blade. A **flap** is the part of the paper on one face after the folds so far: the paper inside one half-plane per fold line. A flap folded off another takes that flap's half-planes, keeping its whole face: a line that would cut into the face gives way to the face's own edge where it runs through an end of the hinge, and is let go elsewhere. On a cube those lines are already the face's edges; on a gable or a flared trapezoid they would clip its corners. A fold line runs on past its model edge, and there it is a **tear**: the paper on either side lies on different faces. The blade stays on the flap it came along. Folding over a model edge along a tear moves the tear: the overhang past it folds onto the neighboring face. Paper past a tear that already lies on the model stays put: it does not fold there, and a cut stops at that tear, since past a cube corner the paper has gone all the way round and that paper lies somewhere else on the face. A cut that splits the paper finishes, and as it does, each piece **folds out**: from the flap on the face the paper was laid on, breadth first, each flap folds over every model edge of its face that the piece runs across, onto the neighboring face, by the same fold as the fold tool's, until no edge is left to cross. The kept paper swings over into those folds one after another, in that order, as the rest springs back: each fold takes 250 ms, and the next starts a share of that before the last ends, none by default so they are one at a time (DevTools' Folding setting, 0% one at a time, 100% all at once). The kept paper is already opaque on the faces it covers, and those faces hide under it as each fold lands, so the patches it becomes sit exactly where it does. The cut's crossings and the paper's edges break each fold line into stretches; a stretch between two of them that runs inside the kept paper keeps its fold, and everywhere else the fold is relieved and springs back flat. The kept paper is the piece that covers the most face area. What lies on faces becomes **patches**, opaque where the rest of the paper is see-through, overhang off the faces and any other pieces go to the inventory as scrap, and the rest of the sheet fades out over 150 ms and goes to the inventory: the drawer opens on it, a white ring flashes around the thumbnail, and the drawer closes. A face patched whole no longer draws its ghost, fill, or edges under the paper. Selecting paper fades only its white outline in and out; the fill stays. **Coverage** is the patched fraction of each face: a cut scores 100 points per face of new coverage, and once every face is covered the model laps as after a wrap. Paper off the face being worked on, which is the blade's face during a cut, draws 20% fainter, and paper between the camera and that face is fainter still and takes no touches. While the scissors cut, the blade's face's outline glows. Undo steps back through the strokes and folds of a cut, then through finished cuts.

A **craft collection** is a shared object space that crafts stand in (`CraftCollection`). Each craft stands at a spot on the collection's ground plane, z = 0, turned about the vertical, and draws as its ghost. A **collection surface** is any other geometry in it: one flat polygon, which may have holes. Its category is `plain`, always drawn, or `occluding_geo`, double-sided and fading away when seen from behind so the crafts past it show through. A collection's `geometry` lists generators. A `polygon` entry is one surface as given. A `room` entry builds a plain floor and four occluding walls facing in, with doors notched into the wall's bottom edge and windows punched through as holes. The test room is only a demonstrator: other collections need not be rooms. It is 64 by 52 and holds the cube, the box stack, Grid Cube, and the three papercrafts. Play opens to the test room, looking in from the south. In a collection you only pan: a drag past a short slop holds the ground point under the press beneath the pointer, and a press that stays inside it is a tap. Only the reticle picks, never the mouse: the craft under it highlights and shows its blueprint name at the top, unless a surface drawn more than half opaque is in front of it. A tap anywhere enters that craft. It flies to its opening 3D view while the rest of the collection fades, then swaps into that craft's level at the same image, with the desk fading in. The level keeps its 2D/3D toggle. The back button, labelled with the collection's name, or Esc in 3D with nothing selected and no cut under way, flies back out to where the collection was left; from 2D it goes by way of 3D. A craft entered from a collection does not advance to the next level when its 2D level is won. Once every island is wrapped, after the lap and the score, the craft is finished: the view flies back out along the shortest turn, and the collection draws it as paper.

A **dynamic grid** is the set of paths a cut or fold may follow in the dynamic grid puzzle view. It is the boundary of the tangram, repeated across the plane. With no tangram piece, there is no dynamic grid. The default period is the bounding box of the tangram pieces, so one square repeats as a grid of that cell size. A **tessellation** is a translation the player draws on that grid, with the same stroke as a cut or fold. A mostly horizontal stroke replaces the width of the period, and a mostly vertical stroke replaces the height; the other axis stays as it was. A rotation of that tessellation comes later. A **tangram piece** is a square, triangle, or circle the player places. Its boundary joins the dynamic grid. The blueprint stays where it was authored. On screen the grid is never finer than 12 cells across the width.

The **ASMR view** crafts the cube freehand. It has two views, toggled by the square cube button at the bottom left: the 3D view of the model, and behind it the 2D desk. In 3D the desk is blurred and faded under a gradient whose last stop is the desk's own dark color, except for the **hologram**: the unfolded blueprint of the selected faces, in glowing white, centered and scaled to fit behind the model. The 3D view opens on the orbit tool: a drag turns the cube, and two fingers, the right and middle mouse buttons, and the wheel pan and zoom, without changing which faces are selected. The select tool takes faces with a marquee, the same one as the puzzle editor: a drag to the right takes the faces fully inside the box, and a drag to the left also takes the faces the box crosses. A tap on a face takes that face alone, and a tap off the cube clears the selection. The hologram is those faces. Each one hinges to a selected neighbor, walking out from the face nearest the middle of the box, and a face that shares no edge starts a new island to the right. The face under the aim fills orange while it is only being looked at. Edges fade in and out as faces join and leave, and the whole hologram rescales. The hologram stays on the desk, at desk scale, when 3D is off, and the desk always shows each face as seen from outside the model. The desk's drawer holds five papers, each a pale color printed with a faint repeating pattern. The pattern is one tile repeated by the GPU, in the paper's own coordinates, so it moves and turns with the paper and shows in true perspective on the model. Dragging a paper out, or tapping it, lays a sheet on the desk. DevTools, opened from the faded devtools label, keeps a curve smoothing from 0 to 1. It is saved and restored the next time the view opens. At 0 a tool curve is the stroke as drawn, and at 1 it is smoothed as far as it goes, with the ends left where the blade started and stopped. The **exacto** cuts freehand. With touch, the blade is a crosshair above the finger, and that is where it cuts. A cut that frees nothing stays on the paper as a score, and a later cut that ends near a score, the paper's edge, or its own start joins it. The **straight cut** drags out one line segment, and every piece that segment crosses is cut along it. That cut is the segment, with nothing added past its ends. A piece that lies on the blueprint's outer edge casts a drop shadow onto the pieces that do not touch that edge. DevTools' Shadow tab sets the shadow's throw, spread, and color, and keeps them for the next time the view opens. The moment a cut splits paper, the largest part stays the sheet and every other part is **liberated**. A liberated piece over the hologram **binds** to it, and it keeps that net as the hologram changes. Its parts from later cuts inherit the binding, and a liberated piece that was not bound yet binds when it is let go over the hologram. A bound piece shows on its net's faces in 3D. Where one piece crosses a fold it is sliced in memory along that fold, and each slice lies in its own face, meeting the next on the fold. Moving or turning it with Select moves it there too. Paper past the net sticks out flat from the face it touches most. The craft is complete when bound paper covers at least 98% of every face.
